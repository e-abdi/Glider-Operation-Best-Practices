    Data Quality Control¶

    Seaglider QC is built in layers, loosely patterned on Argo. The glider records raw measurements and does almost no data QC of its own. The basestation converts every dive into a per-profile NetCDF file and runs a fully automated QC pass on it, the equivalent of Argo's real-time QC. The scientist can then review each profile and override the automated result with directives, the equivalent of delayed-mode QC.

    Source

    Paraphrased from the UW Seaglider Quality Control Manual (v1.15 with revisions to 2023, shipped as docs/Seaglider_Quality_Control_Manual.html in the basestation3 source), the basestation3 code itself (MakeDiveProfiles.py, QC.py, checked October 2026) and its Alerts Reference Manual, the APL-UW IOP Basestation3 best practices note and SGX Documentation, the 2026 IOP office hours on the flight model and on basestation data processing, and operator correspondence. Where the manual and the code differ, this page follows the code. Defaults change between releases, so check sg_config_constants() in MakeDiveProfiles.py for your version, and defer to APL-UW IOP.

    Where QC happens¶

    Stage What it does Who acts on it
    On the glider Records raw values (frequencies, phases, counts), converts pressure to depth for its own use, logs timeouts and warnings. No data flagging Pilot, through the log and self-test
    Self-test Checks sensors before launch; provides in-air reference values used later in processing Pilot / technician
    Transfer and alerts Files arrive in fragments; incomplete dives can be re-requested. The basestation raises alerts for timeouts, CT parse errors, mismatched constants Pilot
    Basestation, automated Bounds and spike tests, corrections, per-point QC flags, per-dive overall flags Runs on every call-in
    Flight Model System Estimates speed through water, which the CTD correction and currents depend on Runs on every dive; pilot watches its plots
    Manual review sg_directives.txt marks or un-marks points and profiles, then reprocesses Scientist

    On the glider¶

    The glider's job is to record, not to judge. It stores what the instruments report, mostly in raw form. The Sea-Bird CT sail logs temperature and conductivity frequencies, the optodes log phases, and the conversion to physical units happens on the basestation using sg_calib_constants.m, not the coefficients loaded on the glider. Those onboard coefficients are only used for the glider's own density estimate (see Sensor Configuration).

    What the glider does provide for QC:

    • Depth from pressure. The glider converts pressure to depth onboard with a fixed linear factor for its own control. The basestation inverts this to recover pressure, then recomputes a proper depth using latitude. A pressure sensor that wasn't zeroed at sea level shows up later as the CT apparently being out of the water. See Pressure Sensor.
    • Timeouts and warnings. The last field of the $ERRORS line in each dive log counts sensor timeouts, and the glider writes $WARN lines for problems such as timeouts, unreadable CT output and compass-calibration errors. A sample that was expected but never arrived ends up flagged as missing in the NetCDF file. The basestation turns these into alerts (next section).
    • Self-test reference values, used by the basestation:
      • CT sail. Put sbe_cond_freq_C0 (the zero-conductivity frequency from the cal sheet, about 2900 Hz) in sg_calib_constants.m. The self-test then reports the measured in-air conductivity frequency next to the calibration value, which is a quick sanity check on the conductivity cell and its calibration.
      • RBR legato. The self-test's sea-level pressure reading goes into legato_sealevel. If it is missing, the basestation raises a MISSING_SEALEVEL alert and assumes a default value.
      • Optodes. In-air optode readings and sea-level pressure from the self-test let the basestation correct optode calibration drift.

    Keep a self-test in every mission directory

    Some vis and basestation features, including the self-test history, only work when at least one self-test sits in the mission directory listed in missions.yml. It also makes later troubleshooting much easier.

    Checking data completeness¶

    QC can only work on data that arrived. After each dive:

    • Compare the size of the processed p*.dat file with the first number on the $DATA_FILE_SIZE line of the dive's .log file.
    • With SciCon, check the per-dive and per-climb directories for the expected .dat / .eng files.
    • Where pieces are missing, comm.log shows which fragment didn't complete. Queue it for retransmission at the next surfacing with resend_dive in pdoscmds.bat. You can resend a whole dive or just specific fragments.

    Basestation alerts worth watching¶

    Basestation3 checks each call-in and sends alerts (by email or other channels set up in pagers.yml). The ones that bear on data quality:

    Alert What it means
    CONVERSION_ISSUES, BAD_DATA_FILE Fragments missing, partly uploaded or corrupted. Usually fixed with resend_dive
    TIMEOUT A sensor timed out more than the threshold (default 5) in one file. A few per dive are tolerable for serial sensors. For frequency sensors such as the CT sail, any timeout is a warning sign
    GLIDER_TIMEOUT More than 3 timeouts of one kind (pressure, GPS PPS…) in a dive
    CT_PARSE_ERRORS More than 3 unreadable CT records in a dive. Hundreds mean a failing or flooded CT, or a wiring or configuration problem. Treat that dive's CT data as suspect
    TCM2MAT_ERROR Repeated errors applying the compass calibration. Headings, and so the currents, may be wrong
    SBECT_COEFFICIENT The CT coefficients on the glider differ from sg_calib_constants.m. Small differences are normal (see Sensor Configuration)
    MISSING_SEABIRD_CAL, INCORRECT_CT CT calibration missing, or legato data found without sg_ct_type = 4
    MASS_MISMATCH mass in sg_calib_constants.m differs from $MASS on the glider. The basestation uses the file's value
    GPS_HORIZONTAL_ERROR A GPS fix's error is larger than allowed (GPS_position_error)
    BAD_GPCTD_CLOCK The GPCTD's clock is wrong, so its data falls outside the dive and is thrown out
    FMS, FMS_DECK_DIVES Flight-model warnings, e.g. a large rise in drag (possible biofouling), too few different pitches to fit drag, or FMS not running because deck dives are in the mission directory

    The basestation pipeline¶

    For each dive the basestation decodes the transmitted .dat files into .asc and .eng files, reads the .log file, runs the corrections and QC, and writes one NetCDF file per dive. Plots are drawn from the NetCDF files.

    • Inputs. Per dive: the .log and .eng files. Per deployment: sg_calib_constants.m (calibrations, vehicle constants, QC settings) and an optional sg_directives.txt (manual QC). The cmdfile, science and targets files are not used.
    • Raw data is never lost. The .nc file keeps every uncorrected variable alongside the corrected ones, so a profile can be reprocessed from the .nc file alone.
    • Reprocessing happens automatically when an input is newer than the .nc file, for example after editing sg_calib_constants.m or sg_directives.txt. To reprocess a whole mission, use Reprocess.py (from existing .log/.eng files) or Base.py --force (from the original transmissions). See Trim & Flight Model.
    • Sensor extensions run after the CTD and speed corrections and can add their own QC. One example marks parts of a passive-acoustic spectrum recorded while a motor was running. By convention an extension never changes an existing column; it adds new ones (e.g. a corrected conductivity_offset).
    • Suggestions. Where the automated QC suspects a problem but won't act on its own (deep conductivity anomalies, possible warm-water pulses), it writes a ready-made directive to sg_directives_suggestions.txt in the mission directory. Copy the ones you agree with into sg_directives.txt.

    QC flag values¶

    Every QC'd variable has a parallel _qc variable using Argo-style codes:

    Code Name Meaning
    0 QC_NO_CHANGE No QC performed
    1 QC_GOOD Trustworthy
    2 QC_PROBABLY_GOOD Likely good
    3 QC_PROBABLY_BAD Suspect, possibly correctable
    4 QC_BAD Untrustworthy and uncorrectable
    5 QC_CHANGED Changed manually
    6 QC_UNSAMPLED Deliberately not sampled
    8 QC_INTERPOLATED Value replaced by interpolation
    9 QC_MISSING Expected but missing (instrument timed out)

    Automated QC, step by step¶

    GPS fixes¶

    Three fixes matter for each dive: GPS1 (on surfacing), GPS2 (just before diving) and the final fix (which becomes the next dive's GPS1). If the times are out of order (GPS2 not after GPS1, or the final fix not after GPS2), the dependent calculation (surface drift or depth-averaged current) is flagged QC_BAD and skipped. The position error is the receiver's reported error, or GPS_position_error (default 100 m). Positions are not checked against land.

    Depth and CT depth¶

    Depth is recomputed from pressure using the dive's average latitude. For an unpumped CT the basestation also works out the depth of the CT itself from the vehicle's pitch and the sail's geometry. A pumped CTD reports its own pressure. Any point where the CT is above the surface (negative CT depth) gets its conductivity marked QC_BAD. That happens in high seas, on breaching during the surface maneuver, or when the pressure sensor wasn't zeroed. In the last case, correct it with depth_bias. Pressure is not checked for being monotonic on the dive or climb.

    Other pressure options in sg_calib_constants.m: depth_slope_correction for data recorded with a wrong pressure slope, and smooth_truck_pressure (off by default) to smooth a noisy glider pressure signal. A legato's own pressure channel is despiked automatically, and the despiked points are marked QC_INTERPOLATED.

    Temperature, conductivity and salinity¶

    Every sampled point starts as QC_GOOD and unsampled points as QC_UNSAMPLED. On the raw values:

    1. Frequency bounds (unpumped CT only, if you set them) mark out-of-range frequencies QC_BAD.
    2. Global bounds: temperature and salinity outside the limits are QC_BAD at any depth. A salinity failure not explained by temperature is blamed on conductivity.
    3. Spike tests on temperature and conductivity look for single-point electrical noise. Each point is compared with the average of its two neighbours, normalised by their depth separation, against a shallow or deep limit. In the raw variables spikes are marked QC_PROBABLY_BAD. The same tests run again on the corrected copies, where spikes are marked QC_INTERPOLATED and filled in.
    4. A bad temperature or conductivity point makes the matching salinity QC_BAD.

    For a GPCTD or legato, the first raw pass runs the bounds tests only, with no spike test. A GPCTD writes invalid values at the end of each record, and the bounds test is what removes them.

    There is no gradient test, and the limits are not adjusted for regional climatology. Override them per deployment if needed.

    Test Default Parameter(s)
    Temperature range −2.5 to 43 °C QC_temp_min, QC_temp_max
    Salinity range 19 to 45 PSU QC_salin_min, QC_salin_max
    Temperature spike 0.05 °C/m shallow, 0.01 °C/m below 500 m QC_temp_spike_shallow, QC_temp_spike_deep, QC_temp_spike_depth
    Conductivity spike 0.006 S/m per m shallow, 0.001 below 500 m QC_cond_spike_shallow, QC_cond_spike_deep, QC_cond_spike_depth
    Overall CTD at most 30 % of points bad QC_overall_ctd_percentage (0.3)
    Overall speed at most 20 % of speeds bad QC_overall_speed_percentage (0.2)
    What a failed test sets bounds → QC_BAD, spikes → QC_INTERPOLATED QC_bound_action, QC_spike_action

    Fresh water

    The 19 PSU lower limit reflects where a Seaglider can operate, but the CT can still sample a fresh surface cap from river runoff or heavy rain. Lower QC_salin_min for those missions, or that layer will be thrown away.

    The raw values are never changed. All corrections are applied to copies (temperature, conductivity, salinity), which get their own _qc variables. In the corrected copies, QC_BAD points become NaN, and QC_INTERPOLATED points are linearly interpolated between good neighbours. If there aren't enough good neighbours, the point is left as it is and marked QC_PROBABLY_BAD.

    Corrections applied to the copies¶

    • Thermistor lag. Temperature is corrected for the thermistor's first-order response time.
    • Slow flow at apogee and trapped warm water. From the start of the climb pump until the glider is flying again (moving at flight speed and rising at more than about 4 cm/s), there is little flow through an unpumped cell. Those points are marked QC_BAD in conductivity (detect_slow_apogee_flow, on by default except for the legato). This check only runs on gliders without a compressee, which is the normal Seaglider. The nose fairing can also carry warm surface water down and release it when the glider pitches up or stalls. That shows up as a fresh multi-point spike on a T-S diagram. The 2017 manual described flagging these temperatures automatically. Current code only suggests bad_temperature directives for such warm pulses on the dive, because flagging them automatically proved too aggressive.
    • VBD bleed at the start of the dive. While the glider bleeds oil and starts to sink, flow through the cell is still building, so conductivity from the start of the bleed to the start of the dive is marked QC_BAD (detect_vbd_bleed, on by default except for the legato).
    • End of the climb. Near the surface, where the glider is bobbing or bubbles may form, conductivity at the tail of the climb is marked QC_BAD. If the data stops well short of the surface, the dive is treated as truncated and the current is downgraded.
    • Stuck on the bottom. If the glider didn't reach $D_TGT (or the altimeter bottom) and sat near its deepest point without moving, those points are left out of the current and displacement calculations. For an unpumped CT, their conductivity is marked QC_BAD.
    • Conductivity anomalies. Bubbles near the surface and fouling or debris at depth make the cell read low, often over several points, which bounds and spike tests miss. The detector compares conductivity with what the temperature implies. Near-surface bubbles are marked QC_BAD (from the start of the dive to the deepest point where bubbles clear, and on the climb from where they start to the surface). Deeper anomalies produce suggested directives in the basestation output rather than automatic flags. As a rule of thumb, interpolate anomalies shorter than about 50 m and treat longer ones as bad. Anomalies that span more than one dive, or that start or end in a sharp thermocline, need manual attention.
    • Thermal inertia (salinity). The conductivity cell's walls hold heat, so in a thermocline the water inside isn't at the measured temperature. For the unpumped CT sail the flow through the cell comes from the glider's own speed. The basestation therefore iterates between salinity → density → buoyancy → speed until speed and salinity are consistent. Points during rapid acceleration (flare, apogee, surface maneuver) are QC_BAD in conductivity. Stalled points are left out and can be interpolated. A heuristic suggests segments to interpolate and stable "anchor" points to trust. Both can be overridden.
    • GPCTD (pumped). The pump gives a constant flow, so the thermal-inertia correction ignores vehicle speed and stalls. Points through the whole dive, apogee and climb are kept, and speed is computed once instead of iteratively. Anomaly detection still runs.
    • RBR legato. The Sea-Bird thermal-inertia correction is never applied, even if requested. The legato gets its own corrections instead: a time lag, a thermal-mass correction and a pressure correction to conductivity (legato_* parameters). The VBD-bleed and slow-apogee checks are off by default.

    The final corrected salinity is checked against the bounds again. The CTD as a whole is trusted (CTD_qc good) only if more than 70 % of the corrected points are good. You can change that threshold with QC_overall_ctd_percentage. Derived quantities (potential temperature, density, sound speed) are computed from the corrected values.

    Not checked at all: sensor drift over the deployment, density inversions, turbulence, and anything between dives. Each profile is QC'd on its own.

    Speed and depth-averaged current¶

    Two speed estimates are produced: a simple glide-slope model (_gsm variables) from vertical speed and pitch, and the full hydrodynamic model (hdm, the unsuffixed variables) from buoyancy and pitch. Speed is zero but QC_GOOD during stalls. Speed is QC_BAD/NaN wherever salinity is bad, because buoyancy can't be computed there. hdm_qc records whether the speed/salinity iteration converged. The hdm speeds as a whole are trusted only if at least 80 % of them are good.

    The depth-averaged current (DAC) comes from the difference between the flight-model dead-reckoned surfacing position and the actual GPS fix. It is QC_GOOD only if the GPS fixes are trustworthy and the dive has data the whole way. It is QC_BAD if a large part of the flight couldn't be modelled (e.g. long stalls or time on the bottom). It is downgraded to QC_PROBABLY_BAD when:

    • the dive was truncated
    • there was significant up- or down-welling: more than 10 % of points where the measured vertical speed differs from the model by more than 5 cm/s. This can also mean a poor flight model
    • the glider loitered at the surface or at apogee for more than 10 % of the dive
    • the expected error from GPS uncertainty exceeds the current itself

    That GPS-based error estimate (depth_avg_curr_error) is a lower bound. It assumes the flight model is right. Surface drift is computed separately from GPS1 and GPS2. The current calculation isn't done for yo-yo dives (several apogees without surfacing).

    Oxygen and optics¶

    • Oxygen (SBE 43, Aanderaa optodes). Negative raw values are QC_BAD. Concentrations are recomputed with the corrected CT temperature and salinity. That's why a deck dive or a bad CTD leaves you with no corrected oxygen. Optode drift is corrected from the self-test in-air values when they're available.
    • WET Labs / ECO channels are scaled with dark counts and scale factors when provided, but no QC tests are applied.

    The Flight Model System's part in QC¶

    Everything above that depends on speed through water depends on the flight model: the CT sail's thermal-inertia correction, the DAC, and the dead-reckoned positions. The basestation's Flight Model System fits lift/drag coefficients and a volume offset for every dive and uses those values, not the $HD_A/B/C on the glider, for processing. The details are in Trim & Flight Model.

    Two QC consequences:

    • A wrong mass corrupts everything downstream. FMS takes the mass from sg_calib_constants.m at dive 1. A large mismatch between dive and climb speeds is more often a mass error than a flight-model problem.
    • Bad conductivity corrupts the flight model. Density feeds the buoyancy estimate. When something lodged in a CT cell partway through one mission, the step in conductivity showed up as a matching step in the volmax and FM_vbdbias plots. A sudden jump there can be the first sign of a CT problem.

    Manual QC: sg_directives.txt¶

    When the automated result is wrong, the scientist corrects it with one-line directives in sg_directives.txt in the mission directory. % starts a comment. Each line is:

    <dives> <function> [<location>]

    where <dives> is a dive number (149), a range (84:90), or * for all dives.

    Switches (default in brackets; prefix no_ to turn off):

    Directive Effect
    skip_profile [off] Don't process the dive at all
    reviewed [off] Mark the dive as reviewed (comment who and when)
    detect_conductivity_anomalies [on] Run the anomaly detector
    correct_thermal_inertia_effects [on] Apply the thermal-inertia correction
    interp_suspect_thermal_inertia_salinities [off] Interpolate salinity where the correction is suspect
    interp_gc_temperatures [off] Interpolate temperatures during G&C actuator moves (for a glider showing warming whenever the actuators run)
    detect_vbd_bleed [on; off for legato] Mark conductivity bad during the dive-start bleed
    detect_slow_apogee_flow [on; off for legato] Mark conductivity bad while flow is slow at apogee. The QC manual calls it detect_slow_apogee, but the code uses detect_slow_apogee_flow
    bad_gps1 / bad_gps2 / bad_gps3 [off] Don't trust that GPS fix. Pair a bad_gps3 on one dive with bad_gps1 on the next

    Point flags on the corrected values: bad_temperature, interp_temperature, bad_conductivity, interp_conductivity, bad_salinity, interp_salinity. Prefix any of them with no_ to remove points the automated QC flagged.

    A location narrows the points affected. It can be depth, dive_depth, climb_depth, time, data_points (CTD sample index from 1) or glider_data_points, followed by at, below, above, between (inclusive) or in_between (exclusive):

    * bad_salinity depth below 10                    % every dive: drop salinity shallower than 10 m
346 interp_salinity data_points between 120 122  % dive 346 only
212 bad_gps2                                     % dive 212: GPS2 fix known to be wrong

    When several directives target the same function, the included points are combined first and the no_ points removed afterwards, so negation always wins. Order doesn't matter. All applied directives are saved in the .nc file. A malformed dive number raises a BAD_DIVE_SPEC alert.

    When the salinity/speed iteration won't converge

    This typically happens when the glider is flying close to stall for much of the dive. Try no_correct_thermal_inertia_effects on that dive before resorting to skip_profile: you still get salinity and speeds, just without the iterated correction.

    QC settings in sg_calib_constants.m¶

    Apart from the instrument calibrations, these are the values that most affect QC:

    Group Parameters
    Vehicle mass is the only one required. FMS estimates the rest (volmax, hd_a, hd_b, vbdbias). Hand-set hd_* values are ignored while FMS runs, with an FM_IGNORE alert
    Biases pitchbias, depth_bias, vbdbias, temp_bias, cond_bias, GPS_position_error
    CT sail sbe_temp_freq_min/max, sbe_cond_freq_min/max, frequency offsets, sbe_cond_freq_C0, sbect_modes (1, 3 or 5 modes for the thermal-inertia correction; default 1)
    Pressure depth_slope_correction, smooth_truck_pressure
    QC thresholds QC_temp_*, QC_cond_spike_*, QC_salin_*, QC_overall_ctd_percentage, QC_overall_speed_percentage, QC_bound_action, QC_spike_action
    CTD type sg_ct_type (e.g. 4 for a legato on a serial port), legato_sealevel

    Reading the results¶

    • In the .nc file, *_raw variables are the uncorrected values (Argo TEMP, CNDC, PSAL). The unsuffixed temperature, conductivity and salinity are the corrected ones (Argo *_ADJUSTED). Each has a _qc partner. Per-dive summaries: CTD_qc, hdm_qc, depth_avg_curr_qc, surface_curr_qc, GPS1_qc/GPS2_qc/GPSE_qc, plus reviewed, directives and processing_history.
    • In the basestation log, QC lines say exactly what was flagged and why, e.g. "Changed (24/27) … to QC_BAD because raw … salinity below bound". Read these before assuming a sensor has failed.
    • The time, latitude and longitude variables are estimated for every sample along the dive. The actual surfacing fixes are in the log_gps_* arrays.

    Pitfalls¶

    • Deck dives produce QC_BAD CTD data. Only pressure is simulated, so a CTD in air fails the bounds. The result is no corrected CTD, no corrected oxygen and missing plots. Check the .eng/.nc files directly to confirm sensors are logging. See Dive Cycle — deck dives.
    • Don't mix simulated and real dives in one mission directory. It confuses vis and stops FMS from running (FMS_DECK_DIVES alert), which degrades the CTD corrections. Use NewMission.py (or MoveData.py afterwards), and move the flight directory along with the deck dives.
    • Get mass right before dive 1. FMS uses the dive-1 value for its initial volmax estimate. If the mass turns out to be more than about 1 kg wrong and you change it mid-mission, restart FMS by renaming or deleting the mission's flight directory, then reprocess.
    • Don't rename the mission mid-mission. It confuses the processing.
    • Downstream toolboxes may run their own QC. Generic glider toolboxes sometimes apply QC set up for other platforms' variable names. Disable or adapt it, and pass the basestation's _qc variables on with the data.
    • Remember the limits. The automated pass is real-time QC only. It doesn't look for drift or compare dives, so a slow biofouling trend or a calibration offset needs a delayed-mode review against bottle samples, ship CTD casts or climatology.
